# Push payloads

The data part of the push messages the backend sends and the app reads, one file per notification type. Both sides test against these files, so a change here must be made on both.

All values are strings, as Firebase Cloud Messaging requires.

Train notifications (`train_cancelled`, `train_back`, `leave`, `train_late`, `trip`) name a train by its line, its `mission` code when known, its `destination`, the `station` it leaves from, its timetable departure `aimed` and its departure as last listed, `expected`. Their `until` is ten minutes after the later of the two: the phone removes them then. The line and `aimed` make the train's own tag, so that what comes later about a train replaces what came before. `reason` says why a train is taken as cancelled: `missing` (it left its station's list before its time and stayed out for four checks in a row), `unlisted` (due by the timetable, it never showed in that list while later trains did) or `announced` (the operator says so); an app reads a reason it does not know as `missing`. At a stop where the line's trains start, `missing` and `unlisted` wait while the line has an alert under way: a terminus drops trains from its list during a disruption and lists them again minutes before they leave. `line_late` says that trains of a line run late with no record to tell why: at least three of those leaving within half an hour of now, five minutes or more behind their timetable, while no alert is under way on the line (`line_late.json`). Trains that left while one was under way do not count: it told why they ran late. It gives their `count`, and how late, in whole minutes, the `least` and the `most` of them. `line_on_time` follows once no train around now is late any more, unless a record came on the line meanwhile (`line_on_time.json`). Both last until their window ends, and the second replaces the first. `train_back` follows when a train notified as cancelled runs after all. Neither comes once the train should have left. `trains_cancelled` tells together several trains of a line cancelled in one check, as when the window opens on trains cancelled early (`trains_cancelled.json`): the `line`, its `mode`, the `station` they leave from, their `count`, and `trains`, a JSON array of them in order, each with its `aimed` and `expected` times, its `mission` (or `null`) and its `destination`; its `until` is ten minutes after the last of them. Firebase refuses some keys (`from`, `message_type`, anything starting with `google.`): no payload uses them.

`leave` is the reminder, a few minutes before it is time to `leave` for the train picked that day, or else the first from the way's usual train's time that can be caught, up to half an hour after: with its `platform` when known, and `live` (`true` or `false`) saying whether PRIM follows the train live yet. A train that has not set off carries its timetable only: it is still scheduled, not known to be on time. `train_late` follows when that train then runs three minutes late or more, unless the reminder said so already: it gives when to `leave` now, and the `platform` when known. Any other change after the reminder, the departure moving a minute or more or the platform coming or changing, is sent as `trip`, shaped as `leave` (`trip.json`): the phone updates the trip quietly. `trip` is not kept in the history. Line notifications (`started`, `changed`, `ended`) carry `until`, when the watch window ends, in UTC and RFC 3339: the phone removes them then, so that nothing stale stays on screen once the window is over. `title` and `text` are the operator's own words, in French, as published. `started` and `changed` also say where the record shown is, so that the phone can put it in one line: `scope` is `line` (the whole line, as far as the record says), `section` (between `section_from` and `section_to`: of the record's sections on the line, the one that bears most on the commute's ride, its stops named without their town) or `stops` (the first, `stop`, and how many, `stop_count`). `id` is that record's id, for the app to open it from the notification; a record edited by the operator may come back under another id, or another title.

`tomorrow` comes the evening before an office day, from the configured time, with what will disrupt the commute during that day's watch windows: the day as `date` (local, `2026-11-04`), how many records (`count`) and their `titles`, one a line, each record announced only once over the evenings. It lasts twelve hours on Firebase's side for a phone that is offline, and its `until` is when that day's way to work starts being watched.
