# Push payloads

The data part of the push messages the backend sends and the app reads, one file per notification type. Both sides test against these files, so a change here must be made on both.

All values are strings, as Firebase Cloud Messaging requires.

Train notifications (`train_cancelled`, `train_back`, `leave`, `train_late`) name a train by its line, its `mission` code when known, its `destination`, the `station` it leaves from, its timetable departure `aimed` and its departure as last listed, `expected`. Their `until` is ten minutes after the later of the two: the phone removes them then. The line and `aimed` make the train's own tag, so that what comes later about a train replaces what came before. `reason` says why a train is taken as cancelled: `missing` (it left its station's list before its time and stayed out for four checks in a row) or `announced` (the operator says so). `train_back` follows when a train notified as cancelled runs after all. Firebase refuses some keys (`from`, `message_type`, anything starting with `google.`): no payload uses them.

`leave` is the reminder, a few minutes before it is time to `leave` for the first train that can be caught at the way's usual time, or later, up to half an hour after it: with its `platform` when known, and `live` (`true` or `false`) saying whether PRIM follows the train live yet. A train that has not set off carries its timetable only: it is still scheduled, not known to be on time. `train_late` follows when that train then runs three minutes late or more. Line notifications (`started`, `changed`, `ended`) carry `until`, when the watch window ends, in UTC and RFC 3339: the phone removes them then, so that nothing stale stays on screen once the window is over. `title` and `text` are the operator's own words, in French, as published.
