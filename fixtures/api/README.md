# API answers

What the backend answers and the app reads, one file per case. Both sides test against these files, so a change here must be made on both.

`GET /status` tells the home screen what the backend sees:

- `watching`: `outbound`, `inbound`, or `null` outside the watch windows.
- `checked_at`: the last complete check, when PRIM was read and every notification sent.
- `next`: when the next watch window opens.
- `outage`: `null`, or since when no check has been complete and why: `prim` (PRIM cannot be read), `firebase` (notifications cannot be sent) or `server` (the backend stopped checking).
- `lines`: the lines of the way being watched, with their `color` (`#RRGGBB`, or `null` when not configured) and `severity` `blocking`, `disrupted`, or `null` when fine.
- `planned`: the titles of the planned disruptions announced today.
- `school_holiday`: the name of the school holidays today is in, as the open data names them, or `null`: also when the zone is not configured, or before the backend could read them.
- `school_holiday_first` and `school_holiday_last`: the first and last days of those holidays, local (`2026-10-17`), or `null` with them.
- `late`: `null`, or today's way home when it is later than usual, on an office day only (`status-late.json`): when it `leave`s and `until` when it is watched, local, and whether it was `set` from the app rather than by the configuration's rules.

Times are UTC, in RFC 3339.

`GET /departures` gives the next departures on each leg of the way shown, and its backups needed: the way to work until its watch window ends, the way home after (`departures.json`, `departures-unavailable.json`):

- `direction`: `outbound` or `inbound`.
- `legs`, in commute order: the line as in `/status`, `from` and `to` (stop names, `null` until the backend knows the line's routes), `from_position` (where `from` is, `{"lat": …, "lon": …}` in decimal degrees, or `null`: the app puts the leg boarding nearest the phone first), `reach` (minutes from leaving home or work to the platform, `null` when not configured: the app counts down to when to leave) and `departures`, `null` when they cannot be read.
- `timetable`: `true` when the ride's departures are its timetable's, its stops not being readable live: a train ride only (`departures-timetable.json`). They are then never picked, their `status` is `scheduled`, and their `journey` names no train that runs.
- Each departure: `journey` (the train or metro, the same at every stop of its run), `aimed` (the timetable's time, `null` on the metro, which only gives expected times), `expected`, `arrival` at the `to` stop when known, `mission` (a train's code), `destination`, `platform`, and `status`: `on_time`, `delayed` or `cancelled`, as the operator says, `picked`, true for the train picked for the reminder to leave, and `usual`, true on the first ride with a `reach` for the way's usual train when none is picked that day: the first not cancelled whose `expected` time is not before the usual one, up to half an hour after it, as for the reminder to leave, and only among the trains too soon to leave for and the five after. A train can be said `delayed` before its expected time moves.

A train station lists both ways: a train is shown only if it also calls at the `to` stop, later, or ends there (a terminus drops trains from its arrivals during incidents; `arrival` is then `null`). A metro platform lists one way and its next four trains. A leg shows its next five departures; with a `reach`, those leaving too soon to be caught from home or work come first, for whoever is already on the way, and the five can still be caught. On the ride the reminder is for, all those that can be left for before the way's window ends today follow, if more than five, so that any can be picked.

`backups` lists the way's backups needed now, in the configuration's order (`departures-backup.json`): rides on other lines that stand in for some lines of the way, shown while one of those lines is disrupted on it, by an incident under way (as in `/status`) or by planned works that cut it. Only a disruption surely on the leg itself counts: not one whose section may be another branch between the same ends, nor one that only meets the leg at one end from beyond it, though both are notified. Each has `instead_of`, those of its lines disrupted now, as in `/status`, and `legs`, its rides, shaped as the way's own (never `picked` nor `usual`), each with its `severity`: `disrupted` when that ride is disrupted too, `null` when it runs. A backup with a ride cut (`blocking`) is left out. The list is empty when no backup is needed, or when the disruptions cannot be read; the backend reads them only for a way with backups, sharing its reads with `/alerts`.

`PUT /departure` `{"journey": "…"}` picks a departure of the way shown for the reminder to leave, instead of the usual train, for the day; `{"journey": null}` drops the pick. Only a train of the first ride with a `reach` can be picked: the time to leave is its expected time less the `reach`. The answer is `204`, or `422` with the reason (`pick-refused.json`): `too_late` (that time is past), `not_office_day` (or a day off), `outside_window` (no reminder is sent then), `unknown` (not a train shown on that ride, or cancelled) or `unavailable` (the departures cannot be read just now).

`GET /notes` gives the backend's notes on its own judgments, the latest first, to look back at them (`notes.json`): each with its `kind` and when it was noted (`at`). A `doubt` is a record of the way's lines that no leg of the way was sure of during a window, noted the first time: the way's `direction`, the `record`'s id, its `title`, its `sections` ("from -> to") and its `bearing`, `off` (off the way), `edge` (at its end) or `unsure` (on a way that may not be the leg's). A `timetable` is a train ride whose departures were its timetable's, its stops not read live, noted each time the timetable is read: its `line`'s code.

`GET /settings` gives `usual_trains`: for each way, `outbound` and `inbound`, the time of its usual train at the station of the first ride with a `reach`, local, or `null` (`settings.json`). On office days the reminder to leave is for the first train from that time that can be caught, unless one is picked that day. `PUT /usual-train` `{"direction": "outbound", "time": "08:07"}` sets one way's usual train, `"time": null` clears it: `204`, or `422` with the reason as for a pick, `outside_window` when leaving for it would fall outside the way's window, `unknown` when the way has no ride with a `reach`.

`PUT /late` `{"leave": "22:00"}` sets today's way home later than usual, leaving at that local time: the evening is then watched until an hour after it, and the way's usual train is neither reminded nor marked. `"leave": null` puts the way home as usual today, whatever the rules. `204`, or `422` with the reason as for a pick: `not_office_day`, or `outside_window` when leaving before the way home's window.

`GET /calendar?month=2026-11` gives each day of a month, in order (`calendar.json`): its `date`, its `school_holiday` (as in `/status`), its `kind`, `office` (watched), `remote` (a weekday working from home), `away` (a day off) or `off` (a weekend day or a public holiday), and `why` it is so: `set` from the app, `holiday` or `cycle` (the two weeks' office days). A day set from the app comes before public holidays. Days set before today are forgotten at the next change. `PUT /calendar` `{"date": "2026-11-05", "kind": "remote"}` sets one day to `office`, `remote` or `away`, and with `"through": "2026-11-09"` every day from `date` through that one, weekends included; `"kind": null` puts them back as planned, and so does setting what a day is planned to be anyway. The answer is `204`, or `422` with the reason (`day-refused.json`): `past` (`date` before today), `too_far` (a year ahead or more, which the backend never looks at) or `backwards` (`through` before `date`); nothing is set then.

`GET /trains` gives each train followed, as it ended, the latest first, a thousand at most (`trains.json`): kept for the statistics, beyond the history. A train ends half an hour after its time, or when the next window is watched or observed. Each has its `day` and `direction` (the window's), `line`, `mission`, `destination`, `from` (the stop it leaves from), `aimed` and `expected` (its departure in the timetable, and as last listed), `outcome`: `ran` (listed until it could have left), `missing` (out of the list four polls in a row before its time, or never in it while later trains were, though in the timetable; a train of the timetable never listed nor found missing is not kept) or `announced` (said cancelled, last it was listed), and `came_back`: notified as cancelled, then listed as running again.

`POST /trains/forget` `{"trains": [{"day": "2026-11-03", "direction": "outbound", "line": "C01740", "aimed": "2026-11-03T06:52:00Z"}]}` takes trains kept by mistake out of that journal, each named by its day, way, line code and `aimed` time, and answers how many went: `{"forgot": 1}`. A train not kept is no error. `422` when the body cannot be read; `500` when the journal cannot be written again, and it is then left as it was.

`GET /stats` gives the statistics of every train kept (`stats.json`): `since`, the first day kept, or `null`, and `ways`, the way to work first, each line of a way in the order first kept. Each has its `direction`, its `line` named and colored as in `/status`, and its `usual` train by its time, local, when set for that way and on the line of the ride the reminder is for, else `null`. It counts its `trains`, and of them those `on_time` (less than five minutes behind their time, as the operator counts), `late` and `cancelled` (gone from the list, never in it though in the timetable, or said cancelled). Its `days`, the latest first, count the same each day, with the `usual` train of that day: the first from the usual time up to half an hour after, its `aimed` time, how it `run` (`on_time`, `late` or `cancelled`) and how many whole minutes `late`, or `null` if none ran then. Before them, its `times`, each departure time, local, kept on one day in two at least, the earliest first, and its `weekdays`, each with its number from 1, Monday, to 7, the `days` of it with a train kept, Monday first: both count the same. Then `disruptions`, from what was sent: `since`, the oldest day the history still keeps, or `null`, and its `lines`, those described in their order then the others by name, each named and colored as in `/status`, with the `days` it had an alert, a disruption begun or trains late with no record to tell why, and the `alerts`.

`GET /alerts` gives every disruption of the commute's lines that is not over yet, for the app's alerts page (`alerts.json`); not those of the backups' lines. `lines` is `null` when PRIM's feed cannot be read; otherwise each line, in the configuration's order, as in `/status`, with its `alerts`: those under way first (`active`), the worst first (`severity`: `blocking`, `disrupted` or `information`), then those to come by their start. Each alert has its `id`, `cause` (`works`, `incident`, `information` or `unknown`), `title`, the operator's whole `text` as plain text, its `links` (each with `start` and `end`, the characters of `text` it covers, counted in Unicode code points, `end` excluded, and its `url`: the operator's links to secure web addresses, `https://` and a domain name, and such addresses written out in the text; any other link stays text only), `begin` and `end` of the period under way or else of the next one, `updated`, and `on_way`: true when it touches a leg of the commute, so that it is notified during a window. The feed is shared with the watch: the backend reuses the watch's last read, or reads again at most every five minutes.
